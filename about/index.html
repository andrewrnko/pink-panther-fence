How we work

Four rules.No exceptions.

  1. 01

    Write it down

    Removal, haul away, post depth, materials by name, gate hardware and stain. If it's in the job, it's on the quote.

  2. 02

    Set posts for rain

    This is the Northwest. Posts sit in wet ground most of the year, so we set them for it.

  3. 03

    Take the old one away

    When removal is on the scope, the old fence comes out and gets hauled off. You don't end up with a pile by the curb.

  4. 04

    Walk it together

    No sign-off until we walk the finished fence with you. If something is off, we fix it while we're there.

Why the panther

Meet Panther.He reads the fine print.

The pink panther in the black cap is our guide. He shows up in the margins and points at the stuff people miss. Post depth. Gate braces. What's missing from a quote. He doesn't sell. He explains.

Where we work

Same crew.Different yards.

We're a fence contractor based in Lynnwood, working across Snohomish and King County. Every town builds a little different.

Call (425) 577-9449Free on-site estimates
  • LynnwoodFences →Our home base, where older ramblers on big lots sit next to new townhomes with tight side yards and shared fence lines.
  • EverettFences →Older north end neighborhoods with alleys and aging fences, plus hillside lots where runs have to step down the grade.
  • BothellFences →Newer subdivisions with HOA style rules and backyards that slope, so the fence line rarely sits on flat ground.
  • KirklandFences →Smaller lots and close neighbors, where a horizontal cedar fence gives privacy without boxing in a modern house.
  • EdmondsFences →View lots that drop toward the Sound, where the fence has to block the neighbor and keep the water in sight.
  • MukilteoFences →Steep lots and wind off the water, so posts and gates need to be set to take a beating.
  • Mill CreekFences →Planned neighborhoods where HOAs often set the fence height, style and stain color, so we check the rules first.
  • MarysvilleFences →Bigger lots and long runs, where dogs, kids and wide drive gates usually drive the design.
  • Lake StevensFences →Lake lots, new subdivisions and acreage on the edges, from privacy fences to wood framed hog wire.
  • SnohomishFences →Acreage and farm edges where hog wire, chain link and wide gates for equipment make more sense than a cedar wall.
  • WoodinvilleFences →Wooded lots with roots and shade, where fences stay damp and posts need to be set for wet ground.
  • ShorelineFences →Older neighborhoods with mature trees, big roots and original fences that have reached the end.
  • SeattleFences →Narrow lots, alleys and retaining walls, where every foot of fence line and every gate swing gets planned.
  • KenmoreFences →Hills down to the lake and wooded lots, so fence runs follow the slope and gates have to clear it.
