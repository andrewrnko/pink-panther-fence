How we work

Five steps.No surprises.

The same five steps on every job, from a short side yard to a full backyard fence replacement.

Step 01

Walk the yard

  1. 01

    Walk the yard

    We walk the fence line with you. Slope, roots, gates, access, and where you need privacy. We ask how you use the yard.

  2. 02

    Written scope

    You get it in writing. Removal, haul away, post depth, materials by name, gate hardware and stain. Nothing left to a phone call.

  3. 03

    Set the posts

    Posts go in first, set for Northwest rain. The rest of the fence is only as straight as the posts.

  4. 04

    Finish and stain

    Rails, boards, caps and gates go up. If stain is on the scope, it goes on with the product and color we wrote down.

  5. 05

    Walk it with you

    We walk the finished fence with you before sign-off. Hold the quote up next to it and check every line.

Questions we get

Ask the crew lead.Not the sales guy.

It depends on the job. Length of the run. Height. Style and wood. How many gates and how wide. Whether the old fence comes out and gets hauled away. Slope, roots and access. Stain. A free estimate puts every one of those on paper, line by line, so you can see where the money goes.

It depends on your city and the fence. Height limits, setbacks and corner sight lines are the usual questions, and HOAs often have their own rules. Check with your city and HOA before the build. We'll tell you what we know about your area, but the city has the final word.

We build where you tell us to build. If you're not sure where the line is, a survey is the only real answer. Old fences and a neighbor's memory are not always right. On a shared line, agree on it in writing before posts go in.

Cedar resists rot and bugs better than pine, stays straighter, and takes stain well. Pressure treated wood costs less, but it is more likely to twist and crack as it dries. We are a cedar fence company. Your quote names the wood for every part of the fence.

New cedar needs to dry out before it takes stain well. Sprinkle water on a board. If it soaks in, it's ready. If it beads up, wait. Stain during a stretch of dry days, which here usually means late spring through early fall. We can also stain it as part of the job.

It depends on the fence height, the soil and the slope. Taller fences and soft ground need more. We set posts for Northwest rain, and the post depth is written on your quote so you can check it.
