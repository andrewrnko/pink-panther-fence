Private here.Exposed there.

Most privacy fences miss in one spot. Plan around sightlines, angles, slope and height before you pick a fence.

Most privacy fences fail in one spot. The deck. The hot tub. The kitchen window. The fence looks fine from the yard and does nothing from where you actually sit.

Sit where you sit

Before you pick a height, go sit in the spots you use. The patio chair. The couch by the window. The hot tub. Look out. Note every window and second story that looks back at you. That is your sightline, and it decides the fence.

A six foot fence blocks a person standing on flat ground next door. It does not block a second story window. It may not block a neighbor's raised deck either. Better to know that before the posts go in.

Slope changes everything

On a slope, the fence either steps down in level sections or follows the ground. Either way, the top of the fence drops as the yard drops. If your neighbor is uphill, their view clears your fence sooner than you think.

Stand on the neighbor's side of the line if they'll let you. Look back at your patio. That is the view your fence has to block.

Angles and gaps

Board style matters too. Tight vertical boards block the most. A horizontal fence with small gaps lets light through and some view at an angle. Shadow box and lattice tops let air move and look good from both sides, but they give up a little privacy. Pick the style for where you need cover, not one style for the whole yard.

You can mix. Full privacy along the patio. A lattice top along the garden. A lower picket fence out front. It can still look like one plan if the posts, caps and stain match.

Height is not the only fix

If you can't go taller, go closer. A shorter screen near the patio blocks more than a tall fence out at the property line. A lattice panel by the hot tub, or an arbor over a bench, can do a job a tall fence can't do from forty feet away.

Trees count, but don't count on them. A big laurel or cedar hedge can give great cover today and be gone after one windstorm or one new neighbor. Plan the fence as if the trees weren't there, and treat them as a bonus.
