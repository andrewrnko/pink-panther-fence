Your gate is a daily test.Build it to pass.

Opening width, swing, latch and support. What makes a gate work every day instead of dragging by spring.

You will open your gate thousands of times. Mowers, bins, dogs, groceries. A fence can be average and get away with it. A gate can't.

Measure what goes through it

Start with the widest thing that needs to pass. The mower. The garbage and recycling bins. A wheelbarrow. A trailer or a small tractor if you have acreage. Measure it, then add room for your hands. A gate that fits the mower with nothing to spare will get scraped every week.

Which way it swings

Check the swing before the posts go in. A gate that swings uphill hits the ground. A gate that swings into a walkway blocks it. Many people swing gates into the yard so they can't be pushed in from the street. Look for steps, planters and downspouts in the swing path.

Sag starts at the hinge post

Gates sag for three reasons. The hinge post moves. The frame racks. The hinges are too light. The hinge post carries the whole gate, so it has to be set solid. A wood frame needs a diagonal brace running from the bottom corner on the hinge side up to the top corner on the latch side, so the brace pushes the weight back toward the hinges. Hinges sized to the gate do the rest.

A steel frame skips most of this. It stays square on its own, which is why wide and heavy gates often get one.

Panther says

Look at your brace. It should run from the bottom on the hinge side to the top on the latch side. A backwards brace lets the gate sag faster.

Latch and lock

Pick a latch you can work with one hand while carrying something. If you have small kids or a pool, put the latch high or use one that locks. If you want to skip keys, a smart lock gate opens with a code or your phone.

Gate check before you sign

Leave a gap under the gate, too. Ground settles and frost heaves. A gate that clears by a hair in August can drag on the walk by January. Check the bottom through the whole swing, not just closed.
