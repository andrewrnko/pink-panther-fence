One bad board.Whole new fence?

A cracked board is a repair. A row of soft posts is a decision. How to tell which one you have before you spend money.

A fence rarely fails all at once. A board cracks. A post leans. A gate drags. The question is whether you are looking at one bad part or the first sign of a fence that is done.

Start at the posts

Boards are cheap to swap. Posts are not. Posts hold everything up, and they fail at the ground line, where the wood stays wet. Push on each post. If it moves, pull a little dirt away from the base and press a screwdriver into the wood. Soft wood that crumbles means rot.

One rotten post in a run of solid ones is a repair. Three or four soft posts in a row usually means the rest are close behind. They went in the ground on the same day and have sat in the same wet soil ever since.

Repair or replace?

What did you find when you walked the fence?

What a repair can fix

  1. 01

    Board swap

    Cracked, warped or missing boards get pulled and replaced with matching cedar.

  2. 02

    Post replacement

    A rotten or leaning post comes out, a new one goes in, and the panels on each side get re-hung.

  3. 03

    Gate tune

    Hinges, latch and brace get fixed so the gate swings clear and latches without a shove.

  4. 04

    Re-fasten

    Loose rails and boards get new fasteners so they stop rattling in the wind.

Panther says

New boards won't match old gray boards on day one. Cedar weathers. Give it a season, or stain the whole run so it reads as one fence.

When replacement is the cheaper call

Repair makes sense when the bones are good. Once the posts are rotten, you are paying for labor on a fence that will need the same labor again next year. At some point a fence replacement costs less than three repair visits.

Also look at what the fence does for you. If it was always too short for privacy, or the gate was always in the wrong spot, a replacement fixes those too. A repair keeps the old problems.
