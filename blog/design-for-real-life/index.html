Nice fence.Does it fit your life?

Mowers, bins, dogs, kids and the garden edge. Design the fence around how you use the yard, then pick the look.

A fence can look great in a photo and fight you every day. The mower won't fit. The bins live in the wrong place. The dog digs under. Design for how you use the yard first, then pick the look.

Start with the daily stuff

Walk your yard on a normal day. Where do the bins go out? How does the mower get from the garage to the back? Where does the dog run? Where do the kids cut through? The fence and the gates need to fit those paths, not block them.

What should drive your fence design?

What gives you the most trouble in the yard today?

Mowers and the strip along the bottom

Every fence has a strip along the bottom that the mower can't reach. Plan for it. A strip of gravel or a mow edge along the base means less trimming, and it keeps grass and wet dirt off the bottom boards.

Dogs and kids

Dogs test fences. Diggers go under. Jumpers go over. Some just lean on a weak board until it gives. Tell your fence contractor what your dog does. It changes the bottom gap, the height and where the latch goes. For kids, think about latch height and whether you need to see the yard from the kitchen.

Panther says

Tell us about the dog. Size, digger or jumper, and who it barks at. It's the most useful thing you can say at an estimate.

The garden edge

A solid fence next to a bed shades the plants on one side. Vines grow into the boards and hold water against them. Leave a gap between the bed and the fence so it can dry. Hog wire or a lattice top can carry climbing plants without trapping wet leaves against solid wood.
