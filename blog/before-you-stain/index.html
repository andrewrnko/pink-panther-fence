Gray wood isn't the whole story.Look closer first.

Gray cedar is often fine under the surface. Sometimes it is done. How to check, clean, time and test before you stain.

Gray cedar looks old, but gray is often just the surface. Under it the wood can be fine. Or it can be soft and done. Stain doesn't fix the second kind. Here is how to tell, and when to stain.

Gray is skin deep

Cedar turns gray when sun breaks down the top layer of wood. On sound boards, a cleaning takes off the gray and shows tan wood underneath. That is a fence worth staining. If the wood is soft, split deep, or green and spongy, no stain will save it.

Is your fence ready for stain?

Press a thumbnail into a gray board on a dry day. What happens?

Clean first

Dirt, mildew and gray fibers block stain. Clean the fence with a wood cleaner and a soft brush or a low pressure rinse. High pressure tears up cedar and leaves it fuzzy. Rinse well and let it dry.

  1. 01

    Repair

    Swap bad boards and fix loose ones first, so the stain covers the final fence.

  2. 02

    Clean

    Wood cleaner, soft brush, low pressure rinse. No blasting.

  3. 03

    Dry

    Give it several dry days. Wet wood won't take stain.

  4. 04

    Test

    Try the stain on a hidden board. Judge it dry, not wet.

  5. 05

    Stain

    Work top to bottom, a few boards at a time, and keep a wet edge.

Timing in the Northwest

Stain wants dry wood and a few dry days after it goes on. Here that usually means late spring through early fall. Do the water test. Sprinkle some water on a board. If it beads up, the wood is still wet or sealed. If it soaks in fast, it's ready.

New cedar usually needs time to dry before its first coat. Ask your installer when to stain, or have it done as part of the build.

Panther says

Test on the back of a gate or a board behind a shrub. Color on a card is not color on your wood.

Pick a color you can live with

Darker stains hide blotches and old color. Clear and light stains show the grain but weather sooner and need a fresh coat sooner. Solid stains cover like paint. Whatever you pick, ask which product and how many coats. It should be in writing, the same as the boards.

One more thing. Stain the whole fence, not just the side you look at. The neighbor's side gets the same rain. Bare wood on one face keeps soaking up water and drying out, and that is what cups and splits boards.

Stain protects good wood. It doesn't rescue bad wood.
