Rain is coming.Walk your fence.

A fall checklist for Northwest fences. Thirty minutes now can save a post or a gate before winter.

Fall in the Northwest means months of rain. Water is what kills a wood fence. Thirty minutes of walking now can save a post or a gate before winter.

Why fall is the time

Wet soil lets posts move. Wind pushes on solid fences. Leaves and dirt pile up against boards and hold water. Small problems you ignore in September turn into leaning sections by February. Walk the fence while the ground is still firm enough to fix things.

The fall fence walk

0 / 10

Water is the problem

A fence that stays wet rots. Look at where the water goes. Downspouts that dump next to a post. Sprinklers that hit the same boards every day. Soil and bark piled up against the bottom. Each one keeps wood wet long after the rain stops.

  1. 01

    Move the water

    Turn downspouts and sprinkler heads so they don't hit the fence.

  2. 02

    Lower the soil

    Pull dirt and bark back so the bottom boards sit off the ground.

  3. 03

    Let it breathe

    Trim plants back a foot or so, so the fence can dry between storms.

  4. 04

    Fix small stuff

    Fasten loose boards and swap cracked ones before the wind finds them.

Panther says

Take a photo of every problem spot. Same angle every fall. You'll see a lean getting worse before you can feel it.

Wind and solid fences

A solid privacy fence catches wind like a sail. A post that is a little loose in summer gets worked back and forth all winter. If a post rocks now, get it looked at before the storms. One new post is a small fence repair. A section on the ground is a bigger one, and it usually comes down in the middle of the night.

Pay extra attention to the corners and the gate posts. Corners take pull from two directions. Gate posts take a swinging load every day. They are usually the first posts to move, and when they move, the rest of the line follows.

What to do with what you find

Boards, debris and downspouts you can handle yourself on a Saturday. Leaning posts, soft wood at the ground and sagging gates usually need a crew. If you find several soft posts in a row, read our repair or replace post before you spend money on patches.

Wood doesn't rot from rain. It rots from staying wet.
