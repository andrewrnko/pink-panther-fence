Questions we get

Ask the crew lead.Not the sales guy.

It depends on the job. Length of the run. Height. Style and wood. How many gates and how wide. Whether the old fence comes out and gets hauled away. Slope, roots and access. Stain. A free estimate puts every one of those on paper, line by line, so you can see where the money goes.

Cedar resists rot and bugs better than pine, stays straighter, and takes stain well. Pressure treated wood costs less, but it is more likely to twist and crack as it dries. We are a cedar fence company. Your quote names the wood for every part of the fence.

New cedar needs to dry out before it takes stain well. Sprinkle water on a board. If it soaks in, it's ready. If it beads up, wait. Stain during a stretch of dry days, which here usually means late spring through early fall. We can also stain it as part of the job.

A typical backyard fence is removed and installed in about two days. Long runs, steep slopes, custom gates and stain add time. Your quote tells you what to expect for your yard.
